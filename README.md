# Coffee Shop Sales Analysis Through AI(Jan-Jun 2023)

I pulled apart six months of point-of-sale data from a three-location coffee chain to figure out where their revenue growth was actually coming from, and where it wasn't.

## The dataset

149,116 transactions, 80 products, 3 stores, January 1 to June 30, 2023. No nulls, no duplicate rows, no duplicate transaction IDs, no zero or negative quantities or prices. Whoever exported this data did it properly, so cleaning was mostly type conversion (dates, times, numeric fields) and adding a few derived columns: revenue per line item, month, day of week, hour of day.

## What I found

Revenue went from 81,678 in January to 166,486 in June, a jump of about 104%. Transaction count grew by almost exactly the same amount, roughly 104%. Average transaction value, meanwhile, barely moved, it actually dipped slightly, from 4.69 down by about 0.2%.

That's the headline: this business is growing by serving more people, not by getting more out of each visit. Both are fine on their own, but relying on one lever forever eventually runs into a wall, either your stores physically can't handle more foot traffic or your staffing costs catch up with you.

A few other things stood out:

**Coffee and tea carry the business.** Coffee alone is 38.6% of revenue, tea is another 28.1%. Add bakery at 11.8% and those three categories cover 78.5% of everything sold. Drinking chocolate rounds things out at 10.4%.

**Mornings decide the day.** The 8-10 AM window is where the money is. 10 AM alone brought in about 88,673 in revenue, close to 13% of total revenue in a single hour.

**The three stores are neck and neck.** Hell's Kitchen at 33.8%, Astoria at 33.2%, Lower Manhattan at 32.9%. Nobody's carrying the other two, and nobody's underperforming badly either.

## What I'd actually do with this

Push average transaction value up, since that's the gap nobody's closing right now. Pairing a pastry with a coffee order, a large-size upsell at checkout, a simple "add a pastry for less" prompt, that kind of thing. Even getting the average transaction from 4.69 to 5.20 across the current transaction volume works out to something like 76,000 in additional revenue over a similar six-month stretch.

Protect the morning rush hard. Have popular items prepped before 8 AM, put extra staff on the floor between 8 and 10, and don't let the bakery case run empty during that window, since that's when most of the day's revenue gets decided.

Do something with the afternoon lull. Sales fall off a cliff after the morning, which is exactly the kind of gap that a targeted 2-5 PM offer (not a blanket discount) could chip away at.

Since the three stores perform almost identically, whatever works at one location is worth testing at the other two before assuming any of this is store-specific.

## Files in this repo

- `coffee_shop_sales_analysis.py` - cleaning, KPI calculations, and the full breakdown by month, store, category, product, hour, and day
- `coffee_shop_sales_cleaned_analysis.xlsx` - the cleaned dataset plus every summary table, generated by the script

## Running it yourself

```
pip install -r requirements.txt
python coffee_shop_sales_analysis.py
```

Drop your own `Coffee Shop Sales.xlsx` (with a `Transactions` sheet) in the same folder and it'll print the KPI breakdowns, save the four-panel chart as `overview.png`, and export the full workbook.

## Tools used

Python (pandas, NumPy), matplotlib and seaborn for the charts, Excel for the final export.
